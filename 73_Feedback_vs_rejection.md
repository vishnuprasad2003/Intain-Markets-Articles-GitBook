# Feedback vs Rejection

## Overview

When someone reviews your work in Intain Markets — a term sheet, a funding request, a pool, or a loan — they can ask you to change it or they can reject it. The buttons sit near each other, but the results are different. A request for changes keeps the same item open so you can edit it and send it back. A rejection closes that item. You can read it, but you cannot change it or send it again. Reviewers need to pick the action that matches their decision. Submitters need to respond in the way that action allows.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is the core difference between a change request and a rejection?

**A:** A **change request** (often called feedback on pools and loans) keeps the workflow going. The item becomes editable, you change it to match the comments, and you submit it for another review. A **rejection** ends the workflow for that item. It becomes read-only. To try again, you create a new item.

A change request means “adjust this, then send it back.” A rejection means “this one cannot be approved — start a new one.”

### Q: How do I know which action was taken on my item?

**A:** Look at the status.

| Status                | Meaning                              | Your Options                    |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| **Changes Requested** | The reviewer asked for modifications | Edit and resubmit the same item |
| **Rejected**          | The reviewer declined it             | Create a new item               |

Both actions include comments or a reason. For changes, the comments say what to fix. For a rejection, the comments say why this item cannot proceed, which helps you avoid the same issues on a new one.

### Q: Where does feedback vs rejection apply in the platform?

**A:** The same idea appears in several places. The labels differ slightly.

**Term sheets (facility agent review)**

After you submit a term sheet, it is **In review**. The facility agent can:

* **Approve** — the status becomes **Accepted**, and the facility is created automatically
* **Request changes** — the status becomes **Changes Requested**. The platform keeps a copy of the term sheet as it stood at that moment, including the documents and the signature state. Your signature is cleared, so you must sign the updated term sheet again. When you submit it, it returns to **In review**
* **Reject** — the status becomes **Rejected**. The time, the facility agent, and the comments are saved. This term sheet is closed. No facility is created. Start a new term sheet

**Funding requests (facility agent review)**

After you submit a funding request, it is **In review**. The facility agent can:

* **Approve** — the status becomes **Approved**, and a funding notice is created automatically
* **Request changes** — the status becomes **Changes Requested**. A copy of the request at that moment is kept. You can edit it and submit it again, which returns it to **In review**
* **Reject** — the status becomes **Rejected**. The time, the facility agent, and the reason are saved. The request is closed. No funding notice is created. Start a new funding request

**Pools (market maker or investor)**

Pool feedback is not the same control as term-sheet approval:

* **Feedback** — Market makers and investors can comment on a pool, its loans, a batch, or a data room document. Open the pool and use **Feedback**. The issuer can read and reply. Comments can be updated or removed, and you can see which ones you have not read. The pool stays in its current workflow
* **Mandate declined** — If a market maker will not take the mandate, the issuer can send the pool to another market maker or improve the pool and share it again. The pool is not deleted

**Loans inside a pool**

* **Loan comments** — Market makers and investors can comment on a single loan. The loan stays in the pool. Unread comments are marked so the issuer can find them
* **Removal request** — A market maker or investor can ask for a loan to be removed. The issuer accepts (the loan is marked **Removed** and drops out of the pool’s calculations) or declines (the loan stays in the pool and stays in the calculations)

### Q: What happens to the data when changes are requested vs when an item is rejected?

**A:**

| Aspect                                | Change Request                                                  | Rejection                                             |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Can you edit it?**                  | Yes                                                             | No. It is read-only                                   |
| **Can you send the same item again?** | Yes                                                             | No. Create a new item                                 |
| **Status**                            | **Changes Requested**                                           | **Rejected**                                          |
| **Reviewer comments**                 | Saved with that round of changes, along with a copy of the item | Saved as the reason on the item                       |
| **Earlier versions**                  | Each round keeps its own copy, with the time and the documents  | One final state, plus the history of how it got there |
| **What happens next**                 | After you resubmit, it goes back to the reviewer                | The workflow for this item stops                      |
| **History**                           | The action and status history include the change request        | The action and status history include the rejection   |
| **Signature**                         | On a term sheet, you must sign again after you edit             | No further signature on this item                     |

### Q: Can a rejection be appealed or reversed?

**A:** No. Rejection is final. There is no appeal, undo, or administrator action that reopens the item. It remains available to view. The way forward is a new item that deals with the reason you were given.

### Q: How should I respond when changes are requested?

**A:**

1. **Read the comments.** They say what the reviewer needs changed.
2. **Look at the saved copy** of the item as the reviewer saw it, so you know which version the comments refer to.
3. **Make the changes** the reviewer asked for.
4. **Sign again if this is a term sheet.** Your earlier signature no longer applies.
5. **Resubmit.** The item returns to **In review**.
6. **Expect another round if needed.** The reviewer can ask for more changes. Each round is kept separately so you can see what was asked, and when.

### Q: How should I respond when my item is rejected?

**A:**

1. **Read the reason** on the term sheet or funding request.
2. **Identify the cause.** It may be eligibility, the terms, the documents, or the size of the draw.
3. **Create a new item** — a new term sheet, a new funding request, or a new share of the pool, depending on what was rejected.
4. **Address the issues** in that new item. Do not assume the rejected item will be updated for you.
5. **Submit the new item** through the normal steps. It is reviewed on its own.

### Q: How does loan removal feedback differ from other feedback?

**A:** A removal request does not put the loan into **Changes Requested**. The issuer gets a yes-or-no choice:

* **Accept** — the loan is marked **Removed** and is left out of the pool’s calculations from then on
* **Decline** — the loan stays in the pool and stays in the calculations

Market makers and investors can flag a loan they do not want in the pool. The issuer decides whether it stays.

### Q: Is there a limit to how many times changes can be requested?

**A:** No. Each request is numbered in sequence and keeps its own copy of the item. If the same points come back several times, talk with the reviewer before you submit again. Another edit cycle will not replace a conversation about terms you cannot meet.
