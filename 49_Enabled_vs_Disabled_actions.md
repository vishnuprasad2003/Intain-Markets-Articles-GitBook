# Enabled vs Disabled Actions

## Overview

Understanding why actions are enabled or disabled helps you know what you can do and why certain actions aren't available. The platform automatically enables or disables actions based on your role, the item's status, and whether prerequisites are met. This guide explains the logic behind enabled and disabled buttons and actions, helping you understand workflow requirements and what needs to happen to enable actions.

## Roles Covered

This applies to all platform users who see enabled or disabled action buttons, regardless of role. The same logic applies to all roles, though specific actions vary by role. Whether you're an issuer, market maker, investor, facility agent, lender, or any other role, the principles of enabled and disabled actions are the same.

## What Each Role Can Do

All roles follow the same principles for enabled and disabled actions, though the specific actions available vary by role:

**When Actions Are Enabled** - Actions are enabled when you have permission, the status allows it, and all prerequisites are met. Enabled buttons mean you can take the action now, workflow can progress, all requirements are satisfied, and you have permission for the action. When you see an enabled button, you can proceed with confidence that the action is appropriate and allowed.

**When Actions Are Disabled** - Actions are disabled when the status doesn't allow it, required information is missing, you're waiting for another party to act, the action was already completed, or you don't have permission. Disabled buttons mean you cannot take the action yet, some requirement prevents the action, workflow is blocked, and you should check tooltips or messages for reasons. When you see a disabled button, check why it's disabled to understand what needs to happen.

**How to Enable Actions** - To enable actions, ensure the item is in the correct status, complete all prerequisites (fill required fields, upload documents, complete previous steps), wait for other parties if needed, verify your role allows the action and you have access, and review requirements to ensure everything is complete. Understanding how to enable actions helps you know what steps to take.

**Common Scenarios** - Common scenarios include waiting for status changes, completing required fields, uploading required documents, waiting for other parties to act, ensuring you have the correct role, and verifying prerequisites are met. Understanding common scenarios helps you identify why actions might be disabled.

## Important Access Notes

**Enabled Buttons Mean You Can Use Them** - If a button is enabled, you can click it and the action will proceed. Enabled buttons indicate that all conditions are met, prerequisites are satisfied, and the action is appropriate for the current state. You can proceed with confidence when buttons are enabled.

**Disabled Buttons Show Reasons** - Hover over disabled buttons or check tooltips to see why they're disabled. The platform typically provides explanations for disabled actions, helping you understand what needs to happen. Always check tooltips or messages to understand why actions are disabled.

**Status Controls Availability** - The current status is the primary factor determining action availability. Status must allow the action for it to be enabled. Understanding how status controls availability helps you know what actions are possible at each stage.

**Prerequisites Must Be Met** - Required information, documents, or previous steps must be complete before actions are enabled. Missing prerequisites disable actions. Understanding prerequisites helps you know what needs to be completed before actions become available.

**Permissions Determine Access** - Your role and permissions control what actions you can take. Even if status allows an action, you may not have permission. Understanding permissions helps you know what actions are available to your role.

**Workflow Order Matters** - Actions must happen in the correct order according to workflow rules. You cannot skip steps or proceed out of sequence. Understanding workflow order helps you know what needs to happen before actions become available.

**Complete Requirements** - All requirements must be met before actions are enabled. Partial completion keeps actions disabled. Understanding complete requirements helps you ensure all prerequisites are met.

**Check Messages** - The platform often shows messages explaining why actions are disabled. Look for notifications or messages that explain restrictions. Checking messages helps you understand why actions might be disabled.

**Status Changes Enable Actions** - When status changes, new actions may become available. Status progression enables new capabilities. Understanding how status changes enable actions helps you know when new capabilities become available.

**Individual Tracking** - Each user's permissions and access are tracked individually. Your enabled/disabled actions reflect your specific role and access. Understanding individual tracking helps you know that your actions are specific to your role and permissions.

**Role and Status Together** - Both your role and the item's status determine what actions are available. You need both permission and appropriate status. Understanding how role and status work together helps you know what actions are available.

**Waiting for Others** - Some actions require other parties to act first. You may need to wait for approvals, submissions, or other actions before you can proceed. Understanding when you need to wait helps you know what to expect.

**Previous Actions Required** - Some actions require previous actions to be completed first. You cannot skip steps or proceed without completing prerequisites. Understanding previous actions required helps you know what needs to happen first.

**Document Requirements** - Some actions require documents to be uploaded or signed before they're enabled. Missing documents disable actions. Understanding document requirements helps you know what documents are needed.

**Field Requirements** - Some actions require specific fields to be filled before they're enabled. Missing or incomplete fields disable actions. Understanding field requirements helps you know what information is needed.

Understanding enabled vs disabled actions helps you navigate the platform effectively, know what you can do and why, understand what needs to happen to enable actions, and work efficiently within workflow requirements. This understanding enables you to use the platform effectively and know when actions are available and when they're not.
